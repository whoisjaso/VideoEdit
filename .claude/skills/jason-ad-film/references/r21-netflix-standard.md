## What makes it the standard (rules)
1. **One continuous camera.** No cuts: every scene *becomes* the next one. Logo → device → profile → player → billboard → row → credits → particles → type → logo. Use shared-element morphs, push-through blur and slit-scan wipes; never a hard cut or a plain crossfade.
2. **Signature transition in the brand colour.** Vertical light ribbons / slit-scan bars in red. It is used about 4 times, so it becomes the film's grammar.
3. **Open and close on the same build.** The logo built from ribbons at 0 s is mirrored at 16.5 s. Use a callback ending.
4. **Silence before the drop.** About 300 ms of dead air right before the final logo build makes the hit land.
5. **Real product UI, cinematically lit.** Profile picker, player controls, Top 10 numerals, credits. Recreate the real interface, not a mock.
6. **Rack-focus depth.** Blur in, blur out, foreground/background parallax on every move.
7. **VO = 8 short lines of 3–7 words, second person, present tense** ("One tap and you're back where you left off"). Each line lands on a visual beat.
8. **Kinetic type:**
   - two-line statement, with the accent word in the brand colour;
   - revealed through the signature transition;
   - a light shimmer sweep and an underline draw.
9. **A new visual idea every ~1–1.5 s**, with one breath (the particles) before the payoff type.
10. **Exit through a dot → line → maker credit.**
11. **Sound:** a dense music bed at a steady level, with the VO on top. Each transition's whoosh is tucked under the music. The dropout comes before the logo.

## Applying it to our work
- **Ego of the Greats trailer/shorts:**
  - every scene is one continuous push;
  - the gold reading ball / laurel is the shared element carried from scene to scene;
  - a gold slit-scan "engraving lines" wipe is the signature transition;
  - open on the laurel being drawn, and close on the same laurel;
  - leave 300 ms of silence before the title.
- **Every ad film:** score the cut against rules 1–11 before delivery (add an "r21" column to the ship gate).
