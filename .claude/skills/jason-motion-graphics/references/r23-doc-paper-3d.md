# r23: DOC-PAPER 3D. Documentary paper explainer (Jason, 2026-10-12: "this for 3D paper graphic styles… a specific editing type that we should label as such")
**Label:** `DOC-PAPER 3D`. Ask for it by name: "make this DOC-PAPER 3D".

**Source:** an investigative explainer about a "Claude Opus 5.5" before/after story. The speaker talks to camera between graphic sequences built from physical-looking paper on a dark, lit desk.

## The look
- **Black-and-white archival photo cutouts** (people, buildings, machines) with paper edges, a slight drop shadow, and **2.5D parallax**: the layers slide at different depths while the camera drifts.
- **Physical paper props:** case files, folders, printed documents and polaroids on a dark wooden or black desk under one warm practical light. Grain, paper texture, soft vignette.
- **Real documents with red highlighter sweeps** across the key line, wiping left to right at reading speed.
- **Big stat type** (e.g. "10–20% chance"): heavy condensed sans, white on dark or black on paper, one red accent.
- **Text typed onto objects inside the scene** (a CRT monitor, a typewriter sheet), then a **red strike-through or red box** on the key word.
- **Timeline ruler scrub:** a ruler of months slides sideways and lands on the date ("JULY 2026"). The tick under it lights red.
- **Before/after framing:** two paper cards side by side; the "after" one slides over and casts a shadow.
- **The speaker returns between graphics** with small lower captions. The graphics carry the evidence; the face carries trust.

## Rules
- **One accent: red.** Everything else is B&W paper and warm light.
- **Camera:** slow continuous drift and push (ease-in-out). Elements land with `backSoft` (paper settles; never bounces twice).
- **Every claim gets a document on screen** (proof-first). Use a real public-domain or public-record source when one exists; label a recreation as a recreation.
- **Sound:** paper slides, a stamp thud on a verdict, a typewriter or CRT hum under typed text, a marker squeak on highlights. Low drone music.

**Use for:** history, investigations, "the real story behind…", case studies, Plutarch-style explainers in short form.
