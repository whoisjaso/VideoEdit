# Theta Identity (women): the script

Same structure and rules as `script.md`: an introduction, six rounds of Wim Hof breathing with
identity statements over the breath and a memory scene in every hold, a slow-breath bridge, the
future-self vision with its sounds, and the descent into sleep. First person, plain words,
identity not ego, scenes filed as memories.

Build: `python projects/theta-identity/build.py --script script-women.md --name theta-identity-women-v1`

# INTRO

~ This is not a meditation. This is an installation.
[2]
~ For the next half hour, we're going to put a new identity into the deepest part of your mind, at the one time of day when the door is open.
[2]
~ Here's the idea. Your mind has a gatekeeper. During the day, it checks everything you tell yourself. You say, I'm confident, and the gatekeeper says, no you're not, remember last Tuesday. So nothing changes.
[1.5]
~ But twice a day, the gatekeeper falls asleep. Right as you drift off at night, and right as you wake up in the morning. Your brain slows down into what's called theta, about four to eight cycles a second. It's the same state you're in when you're daydreaming, or driving and you can't remember the last five miles.
[1.5]
~ In theta, the mind stops arguing and starts absorbing. What goes in here doesn't get checked. It gets stored.
[2.5]
~ So what are we storing? Identity. Not ego. And you have to know the difference, because most people spend their whole life feeding the wrong one.
[1.5]
~ Ego is who you want people to think you are. It's an identity you rent from other people's eyes. And because it's rented, they can take it back at any time.
[1.5]
~ Identity is who you are when nobody is watching. It's built in private, from evidence. From what you actually do.
[2]
~ Here's how you tell them apart. Say everyone in your family needs something from you, right now. You're the one they all come to. You're the one who always shows up.
[1]
~ The ego says yes. It drops everything and helps, because helping looks good. Everyone calls you sweet. Everyone calls you selfless. And ten years later, you're tired, you're still in the same place, and you can't really help anyone, because you never built anything for yourself.
[1.5]
~ Identity says no. Not right now. It goes and builds. People call that selfish. But ten years later, you're in a position to carry all of them, for the rest of their lives.
[1.5]
~ One of those looked selfless and was selfish. The other one looked selfish and was the most generous thing you could do. That's the difference. Ego lives for how it looks. Identity lives for what it builds.
[2.5]
~ The part of you that decides who you are is an observer. And it does not believe words. It believes actions. It watches what you do and quietly writes down, this is the kind of woman I am.
[1.5]
~ That's why affirmations usually fail. You tell the observer something it has never seen.
[1.5]
~ So tonight we're going to show it. Your brain uses the same machinery to imagine something as it does to remember it. When you see a scene clearly, in first person, with your body in it, the hot coffee, the cold air, the feeling in your chest, the brain files it right next to your real memories. Do it enough times, and it stops being a hope. It becomes evidence.
[2.5]
~ Here's how it works. We'll do six rounds of Wim Hof breathing. Thirty deep breaths each. Breathe in fully, deep into the belly and up into the chest. Then let go. Letting go means you just relax, and the air falls out on its own. Don't push it out, and don't stop. Breathe straight back in. While you breathe, you'll hear who you are. Just let the words ride the breath.
[1.5]
~ After the thirtieth breath, breathe out and stop breathing. Hold, with empty lungs. That's when the mind gets very quiet. That's when a memory plays. See it through your own eyes, like it already happened.
[1.5]
~ When you need air, breathe in all the way and hold for fifteen seconds. Then breathe out, and we go again. Before every breath and every hold ends, you'll hear three soft ticks. Three, two, one. Then a chime. That's your cue.
[2]
~ The music is here on purpose. It's tuned to four thirty two hertz, soft and warm, so it never pulls at your attention. And underneath it is a slow pulse at the speed of theta, about six beats a second, slowing down to four as we go. With headphones on, each ear gets a slightly different tone, and your brain hears a third, slow beat that isn't really there. It's a path for your mind to follow down.
[2]
~ After the last round, we slow everything down. Then we go into the future. You'll wake up in your house, train in your gym, drive your car down the coast, and stand on the beach. You'll hear it all around you. Then you'll drift off with the words still playing.
[2]
~ Lie on your back. Headphones in. Never do this in water, or driving, or standing up. Tingling and light-headedness are normal. If anything feels wrong, just stop and breathe normally.
[2]
~ Close your eyes when you're ready. Follow the sound of the breath.
[3]
