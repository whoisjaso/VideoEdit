## 3. How this actually works (the honest mechanism)

Build the whole production around what has evidence. Treat everything else as atmosphere.

**What does the work, in order of weight:**

1. **Audible affirmations the listener can hear and understand.** First person ("I lead with questions") and second person ("You lead with questions") pairs, plus a few lines that use the listener's name (distanced self-talk). They must be behavior-based and believable, not grandiose. Each one describes a thing the listener *does* in a conversation, not a trait he is supposed to feel.
2. **Vivid mental rehearsal.** Present-tense, sensory, first-person-perspective scenes of the real conversations, run start to finish: the room, the body, the question, the silence, their answer, the clean next step. Imagery practice has real (small to moderate) evidence. It works best when the scenes match the real situation closely and are repeated over weeks.
3. **Implementation intentions.** "When X happens, I do Y" lines tied to real triggers ("When they ask the price, I say it once, cleanly, then I ask a question"). This is one of the best-supported behavior-change tools in psychology.
4. **Repetition on a fixed daily schedule.** Same script, same anchor, same cue sounds, twice a day, for at least 30 days. Repetition raises familiarity, and familiar statements feel truer and come to mind faster (illusory-truth and fluency research). Here that effect is pointed at the listener's own chosen behaviors, with consent.
5. **Timing at the edges of sleep.** The night cut plays at sleep onset (the hypnagogic window, when the mind is relaxed and imagery is vivid). The morning cut plays in the first minutes after waking (the hypnopompic window), then deliberately clears sleep inertia with a count-up. Sleep after practice helps consolidate what was practiced. That's the honest version of "programming while you sleep": rehearse right before sleep, then let sleep do its normal job.
6. **An anchor.** Thumb touches first finger, one slow breath, one silent word: "Ask." It's paired with the calm, curious state many times in the audio, so the listener can trigger it in the real room with eyes open.
7. **Hypnotic structure.** Progressive relaxation, a countdown deepener, an Ericksonian metaphor with embedded suggestions, future pacing, and a closing (count-up in the morning, sleep drift with no count-up at night). Hypnosis has real evidence as an *add-on* to other methods, and responsiveness varies a lot between people.

**What is atmosphere only (keep it, label it honestly):**

- **432 Hz tuning.** An aesthetic choice. No proven special effect. Offer it as a toggle.
- **Binaural beats (theta 4–8 Hz, alpha 8–12 Hz) and isochronic tones.** A gentle bed. The evidence that they "entrain" brainwaves is inconsistent. Behavioral effects are mixed and modest. Binaural beats need headphones. Isochronic tones are the speaker-friendly alternative and have even less research.
- **Brown/pink noise and nature sounds.** Masking and comfort. Fine for that.
- **The optional whisper/masked layer.** Quiet repeats of the same affirmations under the music. It's secondary. Truly inaudible "subliminal" messages have weak or no effect on behavior, so this layer is never the main mechanism and is never hidden from the listener. Its text is printed in the description.

**The rule for every claim in the video, the title, the description, and the thumbnail:** say what it is. "Guided affirmations and mental rehearsal, with optional soft binaural tones." Never "rewires your brain overnight," never "inaudible subliminal reprogramming," never "432 Hz miracle frequency."

## 5. The psychology you must weave in (the source framework, condensed)

This is the persuasion lens the scripts are built on. It comes from Jason's own psychology framework (the Obavia Psychology Framework). The creator frameworks inside it (Grassi's IMPACT, England's identity levels, Iles, Haynes) are used for **structure only**. Never quote these creators, never name them in the spoken audio, never copy their wording, and never attribute stats to them. Paraphrase the structures into plain words.

### 5.1 The premise: three questions every brain on the other side is asking

The person across the table is an economical brain trying to answer three questions as cheaply as possible:

1. **Is this real?** (Is this person, offer, number, or claim true?)
2. **Is this safe for me?** (Will I be judged, tricked, pressured, or made to look stupid?)
3. **Is it worth it?** (Is the cost in money, time, risk, and effort worth what I get?)

The listener's job in every conversation: answer those three honestly, with as little friction as possible, mostly by asking questions that let the other person answer them out loud. Then one clear next step.

### 5.2 The five laws of the lens (paraphrase these into affirmations)

1. **Clean facts, human edges.** Facts (numbers, prices, terms, dates, addresses, credentials, what's included) are said fluently, once, with no hedging and no "um." The social glue (greetings, acknowledgments, curiosity, transitions) carries the warmth and the human texture. Imperfection never touches a fact. *Why:* filled pauses make listeners pay attention but make the speaker sound less sure (Brennan & Williams, 1995). Warmth is judged first, competence second, and trust needs both (Fiske, Cuddy & Glick, 2007).
2. **Lower threat before you add anything. Never add pressure.** Remove ego threat, money threat, scam threat, and "being sold" threat, and certainty does the rest. Threat narrows attention and triggers defense; being judged produces one of the strongest stress responses measured (Dickerson & Kemeny, 2004). Pressure creates reactance: people push back to protect their freedom (Brehm, 1966).
3. **Let them say it.** People believe what they hear themselves say (self-persuasion, saying-is-believing). Ask, then stop talking. Never answer your own question. Never fill the pause.
4. **One lane, one next step.** Every conversation has one objective and ends with one specific next step, ideally a self-chosen time from two options. One question per turn.
5. **Keep humans in charge of judgment.** Don't decide for them. Don't oversell. Don't invent facts, proof, or scarcity.

### 5.3 The frame rule: "I lead with questions"

A frame is the shared definition of what the conversation is about and who's doing what. Whoever sets the agenda and asks the questions largely steers the interaction. **But frame control is never dominance.** It's keeping the conversation on the other person's real goal and the one honest next step. A frame that only works if they don't notice it is manipulation.

Frames the listener always holds (adapt the wording per context):

| Frame | What it says |
|---|---|
| **The Decision-Happens-Here Frame** | The real decision happens in the right setting (the test drive, the demo, the trial class, the walkthrough, the term sheet meeting). This conversation's job is to get us there honestly. |
| **The Honest Helper Frame** | I tell you what I know, I check what I don't, and I don't make things up. |
| **The You-Decide Frame** | It's your call. My job is to make it easy to decide. "If it's not a fit, no stress, at least you'll know." |
| **The Real-Thing Frame** | Real address, real numbers, real people, real track record. Nothing inflated. |
| **The One-Step Frame** | We're only deciding one thing right now. |

Frame signals the listener tracks in a real conversation: who asks the questions (the listener, one at a time); who talks more (the other person); hedges (few, never on facts); and commitments (small yeses leading to a specific next step). When the other side fires three questions in a row, answer one briefly and cleanly, then return with a question.

Frame breaks the listener never commits: the bait frame (promising what will be walked back), scarcity theater (fake deadlines or competitors), the superiority frame (acting unimpressed to grab authority), the guilt frame, the straddle frame (promising everything to everyone), and impersonation or misrepresentation of any kind.

### 5.4 IMPACT, as a question ladder (structure only)

Use this sequence for every rehearsal scene. It's paraphrased from Grassi's IMPACT structure, filtered so it never uses guilt, shame, or ego deflation.

| Step | What it does | Generic question shapes (adapt per context) |
|---|---|---|
| **Disarm** | Affirm what already works before probing. Lowers ego threat. | "Sounds like what you've got has held up a while." / "Sounds like you've built this on hustle, and it works." |
| **Permission** | Ask before a hard truth. | "Can I be straight with you for a second?" |
| **Intent: tangible** | A specific, measurable future goal. | "What do you need this to do for you?" / "What would a clear win look like, in numbers?" |
| **Intent: experience** | The current or past problem blocking it. | "What's going on with what you have now?" |
| **Process** | How they do it now. | "Walk me through how it works today." |
| **Duration** | How long it's been this way. | "How long has it been like that?" |
| **Doubt (soft, only on a real inconsistency)** | Curious, not rude. | "I'm a little confused. You said it's fine, but it's happened twice this month?" |
| **One positive** | Finds criteria to keep. Disarms. | "What do you like about it, though?" |
| **Change** | Becomes the dealbreaker list. | "If you could change one thing?" |
| **Impact → IMPACT PAUSE** | The single most important question and silence of the conversation. | "What's that doing to your week?" / "What's that costing you?" Then **stop talking** until they answer. |
| **Gap reflection** | One clause, in their words. | "So you want X, and right now it's Y." |
| **Rationale** | Why change now vs. more of the same. | "Why fix it now instead of patching it again?" |
| **Pre-handling** | Past attempts. | "Have you tried something like this before? What happened? What's different now? What do you need to see this time?" |
| **Pull (future pace)** | 2–3 specific, near, concrete pictures. Pace the *experience of deciding*, never a promise about results. | "When you're there, you'll see it, try it, and know in ten minutes if it's right." |
| **Push (gentle, their words only)** | The real, named consequence of no change. No scare theater. | "If it keeps going like this for six more months, what happens?" |
| **Commitment** | Settle? Why now? Whose call? | "Is this something you want to fix, or live with?" / "Whose call is it?" |
| **Pitch** | Permission, then only the pillars tied to problems they admitted. No feature dumps. | "Can I show you how I'd handle that?" |
| **Self-conviction** | They say why it fits. | "Would that get you where you want? Why?" |
| **Price + silence** | State the number plainly, like weather. Then silence. | "It's [number]." (stop) |
| **Next step** | Two specific options. | "Tuesday at ten, or Thursday after lunch?" |
| **Clean confirmation** | Day, time, place, said once. Zero filler. It's the implementation intention. | "Thursday, two o'clock, at the office. See you then." |

**Tonality (four tones):** Casual (greeting, intent), Curious (logic questions), Skeptical-soft (only to reality-check claims, wording only, never rude), Concern (pain, money trouble, stress: slow, clean, zero fillers). Up-inflection when describing the future they want; down-inflection on consequences.

**Objections (filtered):** never argue the stated objection. Turn it into one you can handle honestly: money/logistics → hypothetical ("If the numbers worked, is this the one? Why?"); time or partner → invite the partner, book a time that works for both; fear → agree they want certainty, then offer only verifiable facts. If it still isn't a fit, a **sincere walk-away**: "Sounds like it's not the right time. No stress." Never as a pressure trick. Two soft asks maximum, then leave the door open.

**The IMPACT PAUSE** is a core skill of the whole video. In the audio it is *taught physically*: every key question in the rehearsal scenes is followed by the "question tone" and a real, timed silence. The listener's nervous system learns that silence after a question is normal and safe.

### 5.5 Ego and identity rules

- **The ego rule:** never make the next step a test of the person's worth. Make the next step the obvious move for the person they already are.
- People act on who they believe they are, so the identity layer (the listener's identity) is the core of the affirmations. The *other person's* identity is respected, never labeled or cornered.
- Identity *wording tricks* are shaky science (the "be a voter" effect failed a larger replication), so the affirmations rely on behaviors and repetition, not clever nouns.
- Affirm before you probe: "Smart that you're getting ahead of it."
- Protect four things in the other person: dignity about money, competence as an adult, their role (provider, founder, owner, professional), and their right to decide.

**Situational archetypes the listener may face** (conversation-scoped hypotheses, never labels about a person; need two or more cues; test with a neutral question):

| Archetype | Core fear | What they need | Lead with |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Skeptic | Being scammed or misled | Proof it's real | Verifiable facts, clean, early |
| The Price Shopper | Overpaying | Respect for the comparison | "Smart to compare." Then the setting where value is visible |
| The Urgent One | Things falling apart | Speed, dignity | Concern tone, one label, fastest real next step |
| The Worried One | Rejection, embarrassment | Respect, no promises | No-judgment acknowledgment, a path to real answers |
| The Just-Looking | Pressure | Autonomy, a low-stakes step | "No stress if it's not a fit." |
| The Partner-Decider | Deciding alone | Inclusion | Invite the partner |
| The Returning One | Being forgotten | Recognition | One true recognition beat |
| The Tester | Being fooled | Honesty, maybe a laugh | Straight answers |

**The listener's own archetype:** warm, plain-spoken, curious, steady. In branding terms (a convention, not science): mostly Everyman with a touch of Caregiver, held with a Ruler's calm. Not the Guru, not the hype-man, not the Magician who promises everything. The trait list: plainspoken, honest about limits, warm in stride, one-lane, curious.

### 5.7 Iles: rhythm, locus of control, credibility, give before ask

- **Rhythm:** short turns, one question, listen, reflect one clause, next step. Curiosity → clarity → commitment, one beat at a time. Working memory holds about four chunks, so one question at a time wins.
- **Locus of control:** meet people where they put the blame. If they blame concepts (the economy, time), give practical help. Things (money, tools), give specific strategy. People (partners, staff), help with their team. Themselves, accountability talk, but only after trust is earned. "Validate the symptoms before you cure the patient."
- **Credibility continuum:** with someone cold, share observations; warm, share experiences; hot, share expertise. Don't act the expert before it's earned.
- **Give:ask ratio:** give before you ask (a useful insight, a straight answer, a resource), then make one ask.
- **Sixth-grade rule:** spoken words, short sentences. Every script line must pass a read-aloud test.

### 5.8 Haynes: one lane, price as signal

- **Pick a lane and don't straddle.** Each conversation and each offer has one audience and one objective. Promising everything to everyone alienates both ends.
- **Price is a signal.** State it plainly, without apology, then be quiet. A cheap, apologetic price says "low value."
- **Offer order:** who it's for → price → what you sell (the outcome) → what comes with it.

### 5.9 Ethics: the one test

> **Would they still be okay with what I did if they later saw the transcript and my reasons?**

If yes, it's persuasion. If no, it's manipulation, and it's banned. Psychology adapts *delivery*, never truth. Hard bounds the scripts must model: no invented facts, numbers, proof, testimonials, or scarcity; no guilt, shame, or ego deflation; respect a "no" (two soft asks max); be honest about limits ("I don't know, I'll find out"); never misrepresent who you are or what you offer.

The affirmations about influence must be about honest questions. Never write a line that suggests tricking, overpowering, hypnotizing, or "controlling" another person.

Reading rules for every voice:
- Numbers are spoken as words ("two o'clock," "five hundred").
- No filler words ("um," "uh," "like") anywhere in either cut. The scripts teach "clean facts," so the narrator models clean delivery. The only "human edges" are breath, warmth, and pace.

**Reference generator (Python, phase-continuous). Use or adapt:**

```python
import numpy as np, soundfile as sf

SR = 48000

def binaural(duration_s, carrier_hz, beat_points, level_dbfs=-40.0,
             fade_in_s=20.0, fade_out=(None, None)):
    """beat_points: list of (time_s, beat_hz). Linear glide between points."""
    n = int(duration_s * SR)
    t = np.arange(n) / SR
    times, beats = zip(*beat_points)
    beat = np.interp(t, times, beats)
    left_phase = 2 * np.pi * carrier_hz * t
    right_freq = carrier_hz + beat
    right_phase = 2 * np.pi * np.cumsum(right_freq) / SR   # phase-continuous
    amp = 10 ** (level_dbfs / 20)
    env = np.ones(n)
    fi = int(fade_in_s * SR)
    env[:fi] = 0.5 - 0.5 * np.cos(np.linspace(0, np.pi, fi))   # raised-cosine fade
    if fade_out[0] is not None:
        a, b = int(fade_out[0] * SR), int(fade_out[1] * SR)
        env[a:b] *= 0.5 + 0.5 * np.cos(np.linspace(0, np.pi, b - a))
        env[b:] = 0
    L = amp * env * np.sin(left_phase)
    R = amp * env * np.sin(right_phase)
    return np.stack([L, R], axis=1).astype(np.float32)

# NIGHT example (A=432)
night = binaural(
    28 * 60, 171.44,
    [(0, 10), (45, 10), (180, 8), (360, 6), (480, 5), (630, 5),
     (840, 6), (1260, 6), (1350, 5), (1440, 4.5), (1530, 4.0), (1680, 4.0)],
    level_dbfs=-40, fade_in_s=20, fade_out=(1530, 1620))
sf.write("binaural_night_432.wav", night, SR, subtype="PCM_24")
```

Then set the track gain so the layer measures about −38 LUFS-S (night) / −34 LUFS-S (morning). It should be felt more than heard: a soft hum just under the pad.
