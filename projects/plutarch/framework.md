## The framework (his ideas, in order)
1. **The Observer.** There is a part of you that watches you: some call it the soul. It doesn't believe what you say about yourself; it believes what it *sees you do*.
2. **Identity = evidence.** Repeated actions become habits. Habits become proof. The Observer updates its belief about who you are, and you start operating from that belief. (Psycho-Cybernetics: we act according to our self-image.)
   - This is built strongest when no one is watching, "only in the presence of God". Nobody's applause is mixed into the evidence.
3. **Ego = a temporary identity.** A mask worn because of an *assumption* of how others see you. It's borrowed from other observers: likes, comments, "you look good".
   - It changes with the audience. The you in front of your dad ≠ the you in front of your dad and mom ≠ the you in front of your uncle.
   - It works in the moment, but it's rented, not owned.
4. **The loop.** Who I believe I am → how I act → how I act → who I believe I am. We act to find out who we are, then become what we act out.
5. **Where the masks come from.** A baby just *is*; it does nothing to be perceived.
   - Then the child realises it is being observed by other observers.
   - Ego archetypes form around what it received or lacked: significance, approval, intelligence, pity, and a few more. Jason estimates about 7, at most 10, the same masks handed down like characters in a play.
   - Adults keep running that childhood script until they do ego work.
6. **Words reveal the second voice.** Out of infinite word choices, the one a person picks shows what they value.
   - "Babe Ruth in the fourth inning" vs LeBron.
   - "My company did X" vs "my team and I did X".
   - If reality is perception, words shape reality.

**The thesis of the film:** what we watched as children quietly installed beliefs (good-looking people = confident people). This film installs better ones, through the lives of the greatest people who ever lived.

## How it maps onto Alexander (all events are in Plutarch's *Alexander*, retold in our words)
| Framework | Alexander (Plutarch) | Modern example (Fable) |
|---|---|---|
| Words reveal the second voice | Plutarch opens by saying he writes *lives, not histories*: a small saying or a joke often shows a man's character better than a battle with thousands dead. | Not someone's résumé: the one word they choose when they're tired. "My company" vs "my team and I". |
| Childhood installs the belief (the cartoon thesis) | He slept with Homer's *Iliad* under his pillow, next to a dagger, and modelled himself on Achilles. His mother told him his birth was divine. | The kid with a Kobe poster over the bed, or the one told "you're special" every day. The belief arrives before the evidence. |
| Ego archetype: significance | When his father won battles, the boy complained that Philip would leave him nothing great to do. | The younger sibling who has to out-do the older one. The need to matter, formed early. |
| Observer evidence | Taming Bucephalus: he noticed the horse feared its own shadow and turned it toward the sun. His father said Macedonia would be too small for him. | Fixing the family wifi the adults gave up on. Nobody announces it, but the Observer files it: *I see what others miss.* |
| Identity secure enough to admire the opposite | Meeting Diogenes: "If I were not Alexander, I would be Diogenes." | The CEO who envies the man fishing at the pier, and means it. |
| Who he was when no one was looking | His treatment of the captured family of Darius: honour and restraint where he had total power. | What you do with power when nobody would ever find out. |
| Ego takes over (the mask fed by other observers) | Later: Persian dress and the bow of worship, believing his own divinity, killing his friend Cleitus in a drunken rage. Flattery replaced evidence. | Fame by follower count: when the comments start deciding who you are. |
| The lesson | Identity built in private carried him to the edge of the world. Ego fed in public broke him. | Build the self no one claps for. |

## Rules for writing
- Plutarch's events are told as he tells them.
  - The framework lines are Fable's, clearly as *our* reading ("notice what happened here…"), never put in Plutarch's mouth.
  - Where Plutarch is uncertain (e.g. the divine birth, the poison rumours), say so.
- **One modern example per belief beat,** concrete enough to picture: a phone, a gym at 5 a.m., a group chat, a haircut, a job title.
- **Plain modern English.** Short sentences. Sleep pace leaves room for the image.
